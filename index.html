Sources. Recipes are transcribed from primary texts read in full: Apicius, De Re Coquinaria (c. 400 CE); The Forme of Cury (c. 1390, ed. Pegge 1780); The Virginia House-Wife (Randolph, 1824); Modern Cookery (Acton, 1845); Book of Household Management (Beeton, 1861); The American Frugal Housewife (Child, 1832); and Max Miller's Tasting History, which supplied the Sackler, Roman, Medieval and wartime material.

Original spellings are kept where the source's wording is quoted. Quantities are modernised for a home kitchen; the source wording is shown alongside so you can see what changed. Where a period ingredient is genuinely unobtainable, the modern stand-in is named with a citation — silphium is the famous one, and it is, for once, genuinely extinct.
